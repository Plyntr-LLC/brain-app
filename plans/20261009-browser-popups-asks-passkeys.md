## Today

- `desk/inapp.ts` turns every new window into a load in the same window (`setWindowOpenHandler` → `wc.loadURL(url)`, deny). The opener's page is gone and `window.opener` is null, so "Sign in with Google/Apple/Microsoft" pop-ups that post the result back to the opener never finish. A load-time `window.open` (an ad) replaces the page. A link in WhatsApp Web (it opens links in a new window) replaces WhatsApp in the shared window, and B1 refuses Back there.
- Every permission except `clipboard-sanitized-write` and `fullscreen` is refused silently.
- No WebAuthn authenticator is configured. `isUserVerifyingPlatformAuthenticatorAvailable()` is false, and `navigator.credentials.get()` waits with nothing on screen until the page's own timeout (probed in dev).

Probes on Electron 44 (`/tmp/popprobe*`, `/tmp/kag`, 2026-10-09):

## Definitions

- **Live, with a person there** (r1 K1): that window's picture is being watched (wide or large, in the tab on screen, the existing `live()`), and Brain's main window is visible, not minimized, and focused when the ask arrives. Used by every site ask and the passkey gate.
- **Input Brain sent**: a pointer down or key from the person's live picture, or an AI click or key step, sent to that window.

## 1. Behavior when done

### Pop-ups

1. A page's new window opens as a pop-up on top of the window it came from, only if Brain sent input to that window in the last 5 seconds (r1 E2; Chromium's own user-activation window is 5 s). Any other `window.open` (a load-time ad, a timer) opens nothing and the page stays. Only `http`, `https` and `about:blank` open. (Found in the build: Electron reports `window.open('javascript:…')` as an `about:blank` pop-up, the same as `window.open('')`, so it opens as an empty pop-up the page scripts. That is no more than the page can already do with `window.open('')`; `file:` never reaches Brain, Chromium blocks it.)
2. A pop-up is a hidden offscreen window in the same session (same logins) that keeps `window.opener` when the page asked for it. Its setup runs synchronously inside `createWindow` (r1 E1, R4): it is recorded under its key (`keyOfPage`) before its first document runs; the debugger attaches; and `Page.enable`, file-chooser interception, focus emulation and the 1100×800 viewport (P-b) are sent at once. Brain's input to the pop-up (person or AI) waits until those have answered (`STEP_MS` limit). Its live picture uses the screencast as the "page changed" signal, then the same `capturePage` frames as every window.
3. While a pop-up is on top, everything that shows or drives that window key shows and drives the top pop-up: the picture, live frames, the face (address, Back/Forward), the person's clicks and keys, the AI's read/click/type/key/scroll/screenshot, the address field, the right-click menu, file asks, print and downloads (reported to the owners showing the key).
4. AI steps never land on a window they did not read (r1 R1). (r2 #5) AI clicks and keys count as input for the 5 s pop-up rule in item 1, so a page the AI drives can open a timer pop-up within 5 s; that is accepted because this item refuses the AI's next click, type or key until it reads again. Do not tighten item 1 for it. Each read records which window (its webContents id) the AI read. `browser_click`, `browser_type` and `browser_key` refuse when the top window has changed since that owner's last read: "A pop-up opened (or closed) since your last read. Read the page again." A step during which a pop-up opened or closed says so in its result.
5. When a pop-up closes (its own `window.close()`, a crash, or Close pop-up), it leaves the stack wherever it is (r1 R3). The top is the newest pop-up still open, else the base window, which comes back as it was. Live watchers move to the new top. A pop-up whose opener closed stays, and still counts toward the cap.
6. The row gains "Close pop-up", after Reload and before the address field, shown only while a pop-up is on top. Close pop-up destroys that pop-up's host (a page's `beforeunload` cannot keep it). `browser_close` closes the top pop-up first and says so; with no pop-up it closes the place as today.
7. At most 3 pop-ups per window key. A new-window ask beyond that opens nothing (r1 E4).
8. Navigation filter on every Brain window, base and pop-up (r1 E3): a main-frame navigation (`will-navigate`, and a pop-up's first URL) to anything but `http`, `https`, `about:blank` or `blob:` is refused.
9. WhatsApp (`wa`, `wa:<name>`): links clicked in WhatsApp open as pop-ups on top (same 5 s rule), and Close pop-up returns to WhatsApp. Sending (`whatsapp_send`, Desk WhatsApp tiles) drives the WhatsApp window itself through a separate base-window accessor, never the top (r1 R2). It refuses to start while a pop-up is open over that WhatsApp ("A pop-up is open over WhatsApp (<address>). Close it, then send again."), checks again just before the final Enter, and aborts if a pop-up opened during the send. Back and Forward stay refused on WhatsApp keys, pop-up included.
10. Closing a place's window (or `closeAll`) closes its pop-ups. A closed window's key stays known for 60 s, so a download from a pop-up that opened an attachment and closed at once still reaches its owners.
11. The AI's `browser_open` text gains one sentence: a link or button that opens a new window shows it on top; when it closes, the page under it comes back; `browser_close` closes the pop-up first.

### The page guard (one preload for every page)

12. Every Brain browser session registers one frame preload (`src/preload/page-guard.ts`, sandbox-safe: only `contextBridge` and `ipcRenderer`), and every Brain page runs with `nodeIntegrationInSubFrames: true`, so the guard also runs in iframes (P-c). Before any page script it replaces `window.print` (B1's print request, now also on a pop-up's first page and in iframes) and wraps `navigator.credentials.create`/`get` (the passkey gate). B1's debugger print script and `console-message` listener are removed. Main matches each message to its window by the sender's webContents id (`keyOfPage`), never by anything the page says. A sender with no key (Brain's own window, anything Brain does not hold) is refused.

### Site asks (camera, microphone, clipboard read)

13. A page may ask for the camera, the microphone or clipboard read only while its window is live with a person there. Otherwise it is refused with no card. The AI never gets camera, microphone or clipboard on a page nobody is watching.
14. Only the top-level page may ask (r1 P1). An ask whose requesting origin differs from the window's main-frame origin (an embedded iframe) is refused with no card. The card names the main frame's site, read in main from the window, not from anything the page says.
15. The ask shows a card under the picture in every chat that shows that window live: "<site> wants to use your camera and microphone." with Allow and Don't allow. The AI has no tool that answers it. Nothing is granted until a person presses Allow, and an answer is accepted once, only from a chat the card was sent to.
16. Allow for camera and microphone is remembered per login (partition) and site (main-frame origin) in `userData/site-permissions.json`. A remembered Allow is used again only while the window is live with a person there (r1 P2); otherwise refused with no card. Clipboard read is never remembered (r1 P3): each ask gets a card.
17. Don't allow refuses this ask and every repeat of the same ask from that site in that login until Brain quits, with no new card. A card nobody answers in 2 minutes refuses and says it timed out. Open cards are keyed on (login, site, exact set asked) (r1 P4): an ask for camera + microphone never joins a camera-only card. Every request callback is called exactly once: answer, repeat-refusal, timeout, or the window going away (refused, and the card goes).
18. For camera and microphone, Allow first asks macOS (`systemPreferences.askForMediaAccess`). If macOS says no, the page is refused and the card says: "macOS has Brain's camera turned off. System Settings > Privacy & Security > Camera."
19. The right-click menu gains "Forget this site's permissions" when the page's site has a remembered Allow in that login.
20. What a page sees when it checks first (`navigator.permissions.query`, `enumerateDevices` labels): granted only for a remembered Allow while live with a person there; denied otherwise (P-e). A site that will not ask once it sees denied (Meet sometimes) never gets a card, so its first Allow cannot happen; answering granted while live (r2 #4) would show device names earlier and is left to the backlog, as Joe's call.
21. Everything else stays refused with no card, as today: location (P-d), notifications (Electron gives main no click event for a page's notification, and a click would try to raise a hidden window), MIDI, USB, serial, HID, pointer lock, opening an outside app through a custom link. WhatsApp Web's notification ask on load stays refused silently. `clipboard-sanitized-write` and `fullscreen` stay granted.
22. Packaging: `Info.plist` gains the camera and microphone usage texts (no location text, r1 S5). The app and its helpers gain `device.camera` and `device.audio-input`.

### Passkeys

23. Brain calls `app.configureWebAuthn({ touchID: { keychainAccessGroup: 'DWYL4KK53B.com.plyntr.brain.webauthn', promptReason: 'sign in to $1' } })` after ready only when packaged and its own signature carries that keychain group (r1 S3, r2 #2): at launch it reads its entitlements with `codesign -d --entitlements - --xml` on its own bundle (async, off the startup path). An unsigned or ad-hoc build never offers a platform authenticator that would fail with -34018. Pages can then create passkeys and sign in with Touch ID. They are saved in Brain's own keychain group on this Mac, kept apart per login by Electron. They are not the iCloud Keychain passkeys in Joe's Passwords app (phase C). The Touch ID prompt reads "Brain" is trying to sign in to <the site's own domain>, so Joe sees the site before he touches the sensor.
24. Signing: the build embeds `build/brain.provisionprofile` (the profile above; not a secret, every copy ships it). The main app's entitlements add `com.apple.application-identifier` `DWYL4KK53B.com.plyntr.brain`, `com.apple.developer.team-identifier` `DWYL4KK53B` and `keychain-access-groups` [`DWYL4KK53B.com.plyntr.brain.webauthn`]. `entitlementsInherit` becomes `build/entitlements.inherit.plist` without those three. electron-builder applies it to every nested executable (helpers, crashpad, node-pty's `spawn-helper`, `hq-sync`) (r1 S4).
25. The pack never produces a Brain that macOS kills (r1 S2, S3, r2 #2). `scripts/pack-mac.cjs` passes the provisioning profile only when it signs with a Developer ID identity. After that signed build, `scripts/verify-profile.cjs` reads `Contents/embedded.provisionprofile` (`security cms -D`) and fails the pack unless its `DeveloperCertificates` include the signing leaf certificate (SHA-1 compare with `codesign -d --extract-certificates`) and its `com.apple.application-identifier` is `DWYL4KK53B.com.plyntr.brain`. The unsigned path (`--config.mac.identity=null`, no Developer ID on that Mac) stays as today: electron-builder applies no entitlements and embeds no profile, and item 23 keeps passkeys off there. The profile expiry, 2031-09-17, is in the backlog.
26. A page's passkey request (`create` or `get` with `publicKey`) first asks main through the page guard. Main lets it go ahead only while the window is live with a person there; otherwise the page's promise rejects at once with `NotAllowedError` and Chromium never sees the request. So the AI clicking "Sign in with a passkey" never puts a Touch ID prompt on Joe's screen. Conditional (autofill) requests reject the same way with no card. Requests without `publicKey` pass through untouched.
27. While a live request waits, the chats showing that window get a card: "<site> is asking for a passkey. Brain can use passkeys saved in Brain (Touch ID) and security keys." with Cancel. Cancel aborts the page's request through an `AbortController` the guard added (the page sees `AbortError`); the page's own `signal` still works. The card goes when the request ends either way. One passkey card per window: a second request while one waits is refused.
28. When Touch ID finds more than one Brain passkey for a site (`select-webauthn-account`), main finds the window from `details.frame` (`webContents.fromFrame` → `keyOfPage`). With no frame or no key it cancels at once (r1 K3). Otherwise the card lists the accounts by name; the person picks one or cancels. The callback is called exactly once (pick, cancel, a 2-minute timeout, or the window going away).
29. The guard is a courtesy, not the security boundary (r1 K4). It runs in iframes too (12), so pages have to work at it to get around it, and the most that gets them is a Touch ID prompt naming their own domain, which still needs Joe's finger.

## 2. How this can fail

F1. A pop-up loses its opener, or sign-in never completes.
F2. After a pop-up closes, the picture, live frames or AI steps stay on the dead pop-up or go to the wrong window.
F3. Pages open hidden windows without limit, or windows outlive their key (leaked BaseWindows), including after a pop-up closes itself or crashes.
F4. A pop-up or a later navigation reaches a non-web scheme.
F5. A pop-up over WhatsApp gets the text of a WhatsApp send, before or during the send.
F6. A file ask, menu, print or download from a pop-up goes to the wrong owner or page, or a pop-up's file input opens an invisible macOS panel.
F7. A permission is granted with no person pressing Allow (AI, timeout, a different chat's answer, a remembered Allow while nobody watches), to an iframe, or to a site other than the one on the card.
F8. A decision leaks across logins or sites; Don't allow lets a page spam cards; a request callback is never called or called twice.
F9. An ask from a page nobody watches hangs or shows a card.
F10. A camera/mic grant without macOS's permission silently fails while the card says it worked.
F11. The signed build with the profile is killed at launch (main or any nested executable), or 0.1.138's auto-update to it fails, or a pack signed with another certificate ships.
F12. A passkey request hangs with no way out, or the AI triggers a Touch ID prompt.
F13. `select-webauthn-account`'s callback is never called, or called twice.
F14. The new button or cards move, restyle or replace B1's row, sizes, dock or download lines.
F15. A pop-up's first page or an iframe escapes the print guard or passkey gate, or the guard trusts what a page says.
F16. WhatsApp's notification ask on load floods chats with cards, or a WhatsApp Allow in one login reaches the other.
F17. (r1 R1) An AI click or typing lands on a pop-up (or the page under it) that the AI never read.
F18. (r1 E2) A load-time or timer `window.open` covers the page or WhatsApp.
